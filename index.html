Chance to make the College Football Playoff

The playoff has 12 teams. The ACC, Big 12, Big Ten and SEC champions get automatic bids, and so does the highest-ranked Group of 6 team; the other seven spots are at-large. Winning the Big 12 is a sure bid. Everyone else needs an at-large spot.

How Big 12 tiebreakers work

Since 2024 the Big 12 has 16 teams and no full round-robin, so many teams never play each other. The two teams with the best conference winning percentage play for the title; the winner is the conference's automatic College Football Playoff qualifier.

Who gets in

  • One clear leader: it is the #1 seed, and the tiebreakers below only settle who is #2.
  • Exactly two tied for first: both are in. The winner of their game is the #1 seed; if they didn't play, the two-team steps pick #1.
  • Three or more tied for first: the multi-team steps pick #1, then the rest start over to pick #2.

Two-team tie: go in order until one team has the edge

  1. Head-to-head result.
  2. Win % against all common conference opponents.
  3. Win % against the next-highest common opponent in the conference standings, working down. A group of tied teams in the standings counts as one opponent (combined record against the group).
  4. Strength of conference schedule: combined conference win % of all conference opponents.
  5. Total wins in a 12-game season (only one FCS win counts).
  6. Highest SportSource Analytics team rating after the regular season.
  7. Coin toss.

Three or more tied

  1. Win % in games among the tied teams. If they didn't all play each other, this step only counts when one team beat every other tied team.
  2. Win % against common opponents played by every tied team.
  3. Next-highest common opponent, as above.
  4. Strength of conference schedule.
  5. Total wins.
  6. SportSource Analytics rating.
  7. Coin toss.

As soon as one team comes out ahead, it is seeded and the rest start over from step a. Once only two teams remain, the two-team steps apply.

How this page applies them

  • A step only settles a multi-team tie when exactly one team is best; if two or more share the best mark, every tied team moves on to the next step.
  • Step c only counts opponents that every tied team played, and skips a level of the standings when none of them are common.
  • The SportSource rating isn't public, so steps f and g are treated as a coin flip and flagged when a scenario reaches them.
  • Total wins don't remove extra FCS wins; no Big 12 team is affected unless it beat two FCS teams.
  • Playoff odds are an estimate built on ESPN's FPI ratings and playoff projections, not the committee's own process. They treat each Big 12 team's at-large case separately, so they can't account for the committee limiting how many Big 12 teams it takes.
