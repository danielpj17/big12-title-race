How the bracket and rooting guide work

The 2026 playoff format

  • 12 teams. The ACC, Big 12, Big Ten and SEC champions get automatic bids wherever they're ranked.
  • The highest-ranked team from the American, Conference USA, MAC, Mountain West, Pac-12 and Sun Belt (the Group of 6) gets a bid, conference champion or not.
  • Notre Dame is in with a top-12 finish. The other spots are at-large: seven in all, counting Notre Dame's.
  • Straight seeding by the committee's final ranking: the top four get first-round byes, seeds 5–8 host seeds 12–9.

Projected bracket

Each Power 4 conference's champion is the team ESPN gives the best chance to win it, and the Group of 6 bid goes to the team ESPN gives the best playoff chance. Notre Dame is added if it's in the top 12, and the rest of the field is the teams most likely to get an at-large spot (ESPN's playoff chance for a team that doesn't win its conference). Seeds follow the current rankings, so a projected champion ranked low today would likely move up after winning its title game.

Rooting guide

For each game this week, the page estimates every contender's playoff chance under each result, starting from ESPN's playoff and conference-title chances. A loss hurts a team's at-large case, and a conference loss also cuts its title chances. The field has fixed slots: one champion per Power 4 conference, one Group of 6 team, seven at-large spots. So a chance one team loses goes to the others in the same race, mostly to teams near the bubble.

For a conference, the number is how many playoff teams it can expect: its one guaranteed spot plus every member's at-large chance. For a team, it's that team's playoff chance. Each game is judged on its own, with every other game left to the odds. Games are listed by how much is at stake, weighed by how close the game is, so a 99% favorite's game sits lower even if an upset would matter.

Sometimes the guide says to root against a conference's own front-runner. A loss can open up the conference title, and its automatic bid, to a second team, while the front-runner still has a strong at-large case. That can mean more playoff teams for the conference.

It's an estimate built on ESPN's numbers, not ESPN's own projection.
